# Learning-content references

The game's questions and explanations are original text. These references support equipment concepts and the design of the training prompts. They do not turn the arcade mechanics into safe electrical work procedures.

Every equipment room contains ten single-answer questions with four choices and one keyed answer. Each of its five levels owns a distinct pair, for 80 questions across the campaign. Calculation examples are simplified arithmetic exercises, not operating limits. Choice order varies with the level and attempt; incorrect answers explain the concept before returning the player to the entrance.

The lobby door order is a gameplay progression, not an electrical one-line or switching sequence. Real source arrangements, UPS topologies, operating limits, circuit schedules, authorization requirements and response procedures are site-specific.

The fictional campaign was supplied by the game’s creator and adapted for the game. Google and Microsoft are fictional antagonistic characters in that arcade narrative; the story does not describe real incidents. The soundtrack is original synthesized music. Speech is generated by the browser/device, with no recorded or impersonated individual voices.

## Character cinematics and speech

The five character rigs, expressions, poses and scene art in `public/cinema.js` are original Canvas drawings. The storyline is adapted from the creator’s supplied fictional arcade narrative; Google and Microsoft are fictional characters here. Browser-synthesized dialogue uses British English voice preferences. The original sound-shape model in `public/lipsync.js` groups common English sounds and facility terminology, anchors timing to browser word events, and learns voice pace from cue intervals. It is an approximate animation model rather than recorded audio with phoneme alignment.
